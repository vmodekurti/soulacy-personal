# Patterns

Strongest first. Groups A and E justify an edit on one sighting; items
marked *weak alone* need other tells nearby before you act.

## A. Staging instead of stating

| # | Pattern | Watch for | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not X but Y | "not just X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "This does not mean X. It means Y." | State the point. Keep a contrast only when X corrects a belief someone actually holds. |
| 2 | One-line closers | "That is the real win.", "Let that sink in.", a final line restating the paragraph | Cut it, unless it adds a new fact. |
| 3 | Sayings that sound deep | "the real question is", "at its core", "X is the language of Y" | Replace with the specific claim. |
| 4 | Staged run-up | "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Honestly?" as an opener | Start with the point. |
| 5 | Arguing with no one | "To be clear", "I'm not saying", "A tempting approach would be…" with no one making that case | Delete the defence; keep any real claim inside it. |

> It's not just a phone, it's a statement. That's the real win.
> → The phone's design gets noticed.

## B. Rhythm by rule

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Forced threes ("innovation, inspiration, and insights"; three parallel sentences then a summary) | Use the number of items the meaning has. |
| 7 | Same sentence opening repeated ("She… She… She…") | Merge, or lead with the action. |
| 8 | Dashes as the universal connector | No em or en dashes in the final text unless the person's sample uses them; use a period, comma, colon or parentheses. One dash is *weak alone*. |
| 9 | Stacked qualifiers ("could potentially possibly") | Keep one qualifier, and only if the doubt is real. |
| 10 | Hyphens after the noun ("the plan is long-term") | Hyphen before the noun only. |
| 11 | Hidden actor, missing subject ("No setup needed.") | Say who does what. *Weak alone.* |

## C. Inflation and borrowed authority

| # | Pattern | Watch for | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Stock AI words | additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, intricate, landscape (figurative), leverage, pivotal, robust, seamless, showcase, testament, underscore, vibrant | Plain words. A cluster matters more than one. |
| 13 | Inflated significance | "marking a pivotal moment", "enduring legacy", "the future looks bright" send-offs | Keep the fact, drop the importance claim; end on the last concrete fact. |
| 14 | Vague association | "associated with", "linked to", "in connection with" | Name the actual relationship. |
| 15 | Shallow -ing riders | "…, highlighting its importance", "…, reflecting the community's values" | Cut the rider unless the source supports it. |
| 16 | Sales language | nestled, breathtaking, rich heritage, must-visit, renowned, stunning | Say what the thing is. |
| 17 | Borrowed authority | "experts say", "industry reports", lists of outlets that "featured" someone | Name the source and what it said, or drop it. |
| 18 | Avoiding is/are/has | serves as, stands as, boasts, features | Use is, are, has. |

> Nestled in the heart of the valley, the inn boasts a rich heritage, showcasing timeless charm.
> → The inn is in the valley and has been open since 1890.  (only if the source gives the date)

## D. Formatting by rule

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Bold on every item; lists of **Label:** sentences | Remove decoration; turn label lists into prose when labels add nothing. |
| 20 | Title Case Headings, emojis and arrows as decoration, rules between every section | Sentence-case headings; drop decoration. |
| 21 | Curly quotes where the format uses straight ones | Straighten. *Weak alone.* |

## E. Leftovers from the chat and the draft

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Chatbot residue: "Great question!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if…" | Remove the wrapper, keep the content. |
| 23 | Knowledge-limit disclaimers and guesses ("not widely documented, but likely…") | Say what the source does not show, or cut it. Never fill the gap with a guess. |
| 24 | A heading restated in the first sentence | Remove the restatement. |
| 25 | Writing about the document ("This section explores…") | Write about the subject. |

## F. Writing for the wrong reader

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 26 | Re-explaining what the reader already knows, so the answer arrives last | Lead with what they need: the decision, the answer, the ask. |
